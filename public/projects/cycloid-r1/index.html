02 / Evolution

From planetary gears to a cycloid.

Each step was printed and run before the next one was designed. The earlier gearboxes were built for the STS3215 servo joints of Bitzy; Cycloid R1 is the first gearbox in this line with measured performance.

September 2026 / Printed and run

G9 planetary test box, 9:1

A two-stage planetary (24/12/48 teeth, module 1.25, 25° pressure angle) with printed pins and journals, hand-cranked. It assembled and ran as intended on September 20, 2026. No backlash or torque measurements were recorded.

October 2026 / Printed and run

Gearbox V2 actuator, 9.97:1

A common actuator for the STS3215: two double-helical stages with prime tooth counts (19/11/41, 15° helix), PAHT-CF gears, a 6708 output bearing and 693ZZ bearings in the first-stage planets. Printed, assembled and turned through a full output revolution. Torque, efficiency and backlash were not measured.

October 2026 / Built and bench-tested

Cycloid R1, 25:1

The switch to a cycloid had two reasons: rounded cycloid discs print more reliably than sharp gear teeth, and a cycloid gives a higher reduction in a smaller package. One stage replaces two, driven by a NEMA17 stepper with a magnetic encoder on the output.

03 / Design

How it works.

The motor turns an eccentric cam, so each disc orbits 1 mm off centre instead of spinning. Its 25 lobes sit between 26 ring pins; because there is one more pin than lobes, every motor turn rolls the disc back by one lobe, 1/25 of a turn. Six output pins with 684 rollers ride in oversized holes in the disc, passing on the slow rotation but not the wobble.

Two discs run 180° apart to balance each other. That only meshes when the pin count is even, which the model was checked against (21 pins fails, 24 and 26 pass).

The disc profile is computed from the cycloid equations and drawn as a dimension-driven spline, 24 points per lobe placed by arc length, so the shape stays exact when any parameter changes. Every dimension in the Fusion model is a named parameter, and every bought part (bearings, dowels, motor, encoder) uses caliper-measured values.

Designed for printing.

All printed parts are PETG-CF. Fits are specified as printed, with an allowance for how much holes shrink on this printer. A printed fit coupon tests each fit at three sizes. Every wall was checked to be at least two nozzle widths thick.

  • Lost motion follows the clearancesThe first discs (0.2 mm clearance to the pins) measured 71′ mean. Tightened R2 discs (0.1 mm) measured 57′, against 53′ calculated from the pin and output-hole clearances. The close match suggests the designed clearances set most of the play.
  • Accuracy is limited by the encoder alignmentAn uncalibrated sweep showed about 2.2° peak to peak, mostly an error repeating twice per turn. That pattern comes from the magnet and sensor alignment, not the gears, and moved when the encoder bracket was refitted. A harmonic calibration removes it.
  • Remaining error is the gear meshAfter calibration about 20′ RMS remains, repeating 21–26 times per turn: the lobe and pin pitch. This is real output error, most likely from the printed accuracy of the lobes and pins, which is why the closed loop measures the output directly.
  • Not yet measuredTorque, stall torque, efficiency, heat and wear. Load testing on a dynamometer is the next stage.
05 / Control and test bench

Closing the loop through the backlash.

The bench is an ESP32-S3 running custom firmware: a 50 kHz step generator, a 1 kHz motion loop that reads the output encoder every millisecond, and a browser dashboard with live charts, automated tests, full data logs and an animated view of the discs moving against the pins.

With the loop closed on the output encoder, each move is corrected until the measured output is within a deadband of the target. The first version hunted, because its deadband was only about one encoder count. Filtering the encoder and adding hysteresis fixed it: the output now settles about 0.3′ from target and stays still.

Driving the loop hard exposed a stall at about 760–900 motor rpm, where the loop kept pushing a motor that had stopped. The firmware now caps motor speed and adds encoder-based stall detection with a smooth stop; those safeguards have not yet been exercised on hardware. Correction during moves, not just after them, has been written and simulated; it is not yet verified on hardware.

What the closed loop does and does not show.

2′ lost motion with the loop on is a control result: the play is still in the gearbox, and the controller corrects for it at rest. Under changing external load the physical backlash still matters, and that has not been tested.

06 / Status

Built, measured, and next under load.

Cycloid R1 is built and running on the bench. Ratio, repeatability, lost motion, accuracy and speed limits are measured without load. Next is a dynamometer to measure torque, efficiency, stiffness, heat and wear, followed by a material comparison and use as a joint in the next Bitzy arm.
